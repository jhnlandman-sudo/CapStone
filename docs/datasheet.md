## Part A — Shared answers

### Structure of the data (all functions)

- **Inputs:** each function takes a vector *x* in the unit cube [0, 1]^d. Initial inputs were supplied as
  NumPy arrays of shape (n, d); outputs as shape (n,).
- **Outputs:** one real number per evaluation, to be **maximised**. Scales differ by four orders of
  magnitude (F3 lies between −0.4 and −0.03; F5 reaches 8,662), so functions are never pooled or compared on
  raw *y*.
- **Growth:** one new point per function per round, 12 scored rounds, so each function gained 12 points
  (plus the round-13 query, whose result is not in the data). The portal files are cumulative.
- **Files:**
  - `data/raw/initial/function_<i>/initial_inputs.npy`, `initial_outputs.npy` — as supplied, never edited.
  - `data/raw/portal/inputs.txt`, `outputs.txt` — as downloaded, never edited.
  - `data/processed/function_<i>.csv` (`source`, `round`, `x1…xd`, `y`) and `summary.csv`, regenerated by
    `python src/build_dataset.py`.
- **Size:** about 270 rows in total, under 1 MB, so everything is stored in the repository. There are no
  large external datasets to link.
- **Sampling bias:** portal points are *not* independent draws. Each was chosen using all earlier points,
  and most lie close to each function's best point. The data describe the basins found, not the whole
  function.

### Data handling and preprocessing (all functions)

1. **Rescaling.** Inputs were not rescaled: they already lie in [0, 1]. Outputs were standardised inside
   the GP (`normalize_y=True`). F5 was modelled on log *y*, because its outputs are strictly positive and
   span four orders of magnitude; stored values are never transformed.
2. **Surrogate models.** A Gaussian process (scikit-learn `GaussianProcessRegressor`) per function, with
   one lengthscale per input (ARD). Closed-form local models were used alongside it: three-point
   quadratics along one coordinate, and an isotropic Gaussian fit for F1.
3. **Surrogate preprocessing and tuning.**
   - **Kernel:** RBF or Matérn-5/2, times a constant, plus a white-noise term so the GP can fit noise.
   - **Tuning:** from round 7, `src/tune.py` chose the kernel family and the lengthscale floor (0.03, 0.06,
     0.15 or 0.30) per function by 5-fold cross-validated RMSE, re-run every round.
   - **Fitting:** kernel hyperparameters are fitted by maximum likelihood with 25 restarts and
     `random_state=0`, so fits are reproducible.
4. **Outliers.** Nothing was removed. Low readings were kept because they bound the peak, and repeated
   readings were kept because they are the only direct measurement of noise. Instead of dropping points, I
   changed how they were used: on noisy functions, decisions were made on the average of repeated readings.

### Ethical, practical and general considerations (all functions)

1. **Real-world relevance.** Each function stands in for an expensive real evaluation: a lab experiment,
   a production trial or a long model-training run. In my own field, calibrating an actuarial projection
   model, where one run takes minutes to hours, is the same problem.
2. **Limits of synthetic functions.** The functions are fixed, stationary and box-bounded, and the scoring
   is "best value seen". Real problems usually have constraints, costs that differ between evaluations,
   drift over time, and a requirement that the recommended setting works on average, not just once.
3. **Scaling.** The method suits up to about 10 dimensions and budgets of tens of evaluations. GP fitting
   scales as n³, and coverage becomes thin quickly as dimension grows: at d = 8 the nearest data point to a
   random input is typically 0.6 away. Larger problems would need structural assumptions (additive or
   low-rank models), batch acquisition, or simpler population methods.
4. **Pitfalls for a future user.**
   - Do not treat a single reading on F2, F3 or F6 as exact; they are noisy.
   - Do not treat "converged" as "global optimum"; the search concentrated near the basins found.
   - Do not use the GP's predicted *level* far from data; use it to rank candidates.
   - The move type chosen each round was a documented human judgement, so the numbers are reproducible but
     the policy is subjective.
   - The functions and initial data belong to the programme; reuse beyond education should respect its terms.

## Part B — Function sheets

Scenario descriptions follow the challenge brief. All functions are maximised.

### Function 1 — radiation source detection (2D)

**Overview.** 2D input (a location); 10 initial points; output is the measured signal from a radiation
source, which is close to zero everywhere except near a source.

**Nature of the data.** Nine of the ten initial readings were effectively zero (best 7.7e-16), so a GP on raw
*y* saw a flat surface. The function is noise-free: the optimum returned exactly 2.0 six times. It is
sharply peaked (Gaussian width about 0.014) and possibly multimodal; a second, weaker source was suggested
by three initial points.

**Strategy.** Log-magnitude triangulation instead of a GP: for a Gaussian source, log *y* is quadratic in
distance from the centre, so even near-zero tails locate the source. Round 1 hit the main source (1.995).
Round 2 explored the suspected second source at (0.37, 0.38), which returned zero, so exploration stopped.
Rounds 3–6 refined the centre, and a closed-form Gaussian fit placed it at (0.628541, 0.628541).

**Learning.** The most informative points were the near-zero tails: their *ratios* carried the location.
Restarting, I would query the triangulated centre in round 1 and skip the second-source test.

**Result.** Best 2.0 at **0.628541-0.628541**. Confidence that this is the global maximum: high. The
Gaussian model fits all near-peak readings exactly, and repeats are identical.

### Function 2 — noisy log-likelihood (2D)

**Overview.** 2D input; 10 initial points; output is a noisy log-likelihood (higher is better), as from
evaluating a model's fit.

**Nature of the data.** Noisy. One input returned 0.650, 0.726, 0.598 and 0.460 (mean 0.608, sd 0.11). The
landscape is a narrow ridge in x1 near 0.68; x2 carries only about 2% of the GP relevance.

**Strategy.** GP-UCB early; then quadratic fits along x1 and x2; from round 9, exact repeats to measure
noise. The final round picked the point with the best mean minus standard error.

**Learning.** Three confident curve fits (rounds 6–8, and the x1 ridge vertex in round 11, which returned
0.463 against 0.614 predicted) failed because they were fitted to noise. The repeats were the most
informative queries. Restarting, I would repeat the best initial point in round 1 to measure noise
before fitting anything.

**Result.** Best single reading 0.726 at **0.682637-0.926564**; best-evidenced value is the mean, 0.608. Confidence
is moderate: the ridge location is well established, but noise of ±0.1 hides any local improvement smaller
than that.

### Function 3 — drug discovery, three compounds (3D)

**Overview.** 3D input (compound amounts); 15 initial points; output is the negative of an
adverse-reaction score, so values closer to zero are better.

**Nature of the data.** Mildly noisy: a repeat of −0.0258 returned −0.0464. Almost all GP relevance is
in x3; x1 and x2 are flat near the peak but not across the whole box. A global UCB step in round 6, which
assumed they were flat everywhere, returned −0.075. The landscape near the best region is flat, with
differences of the same size as the noise.

**Strategy.** GP-UCB; then single-coordinate quadratics; one deliberate global exploration step; then the
GP posterior-mean maximiser in a small radius (0.05, then 0.03).

**Learning.** The record from round 1 (−0.0258) turned out to be partly luck (repeat mean −0.036). The
GP posterior mean, not curve fitting, produced the final improvement (−0.0250, round 11).

**Result.** Best −0.0250 at **0.444791-0.562256-0.859194**. Confidence: moderate to low. The region is
flat to within noise, so the true optimum could differ by a similar amount.

### Function 4 — warehouse product placement (4D)

**Overview.** 4D input (placement parameters); 30 initial points; output is a performance score against a
reference, where higher is better.

**Nature of the data.** Noise-free in practice, with a very sharp single peak. The curvature along x4 is about
−2,700, so a step of 0.005 can cost 0.07. All initial points were strongly negative (best −4.03).

**Strategy.** GP-UCB found the basin in round 1 (0.553). After that, only single-coordinate moves with
three-point quadratic vertices, because joint moves overshoot a peak this narrow.

**Learning.** Every coordinate that was pushed beyond its fitted vertex lost value, which confirmed a
single sharp peak. The x4 vertex predicted 0.7431 and returned 0.7440.

**Result.** Best 0.744 at **0.415962-0.417500-0.363817-0.416634**. Confidence: fairly high for the local
peak, since all four coordinates are bracketed. The GP over-smooths this peak, so its uncertainty is not
a reliable guide here.

### Function 5 — chemical process yield (4D)

**Overview.** 4D input (process settings); 20 initial points; output is yield, described in the brief as
unimodal.

**Nature of the data.** Noise-free: the optimum returned 8,662.405 five times. Outputs span 0.1 to 8,662,
so the GP was fitted to log *y*.

**Strategy.** Trust-region GP on log *y*, raising one input at a time. Every input improved each time it
was pushed upwards, so in round 7 I tested the hypothesis that the optimum lies at the corner
(1, 1, 1, 1).

**Learning.** The corner returned 8,662 against 4,718 (+84%). A probe at x2 = 0.9 fell to 7,073, which
confirmed it. Restarting, I would test the boundary as soon as the monotone pattern appeared.

**Result.** Best 8,662.405 at **0.999999-0.999999-0.999999-0.999999** (the portal's upper bound). Confidence: high,
given the unimodal brief, the monotone response and the falling probe.

### Function 6 — recipe quality, five ingredients (5D)

**Overview.** 5D input (ingredient proportions); 20 initial points; output is a negative score, so values
closer to zero are better.

**Nature of the data.** Noisy (a repeat of −0.061 returned −0.088) and with a narrow peak: points 0.03–0.05
away return −0.26 to −0.29. The early search sat in a poorer region (−0.58) until a five-coordinate move
found the current basin.

**Strategy.** GP-UCB with interior margins; then a five-coordinate trust-region UCB move (−0.439) and a
tuned-GP UCB move (−0.061), the two largest gains; then repeats and single-coordinate brackets on x2.

**Learning.** The two UCB successes were followed by a UCB miss (−0.293), which showed the peak is narrow.
Three x2 readings then bracketed it: the vertex sits at the current point.

**Result.** Best −0.061 at **0.450943-0.365418-0.636307-0.737863-0.002595**; repeat mean −0.074. Confidence: moderate.
The local peak is bracketed, but the function may be multimodal and large parts of the cube are unexplored.

### Function 7 — ML model, six hyperparameters (6D)

**Overview.** 6D input (scaled hyperparameters); 30 initial points; output is a model performance score.

**Nature of the data.** Smooth and noise-free in practice; the GP's line predictions were accurate to
about 0.001. Relevance is concentrated in x5, x4 and x6; x1 and x3 together carry about 6%.

**Strategy.** GP-UCB, then trust-region UCB, then mainly single-coordinate moves along the relevant
inputs, with one successful GP step in a small trust region (round 10, +0.133).

**Learning.** Single-coordinate gains were steady but diminishing (+0.106, +0.075, +0.046). A second
six-coordinate GP step (round 11) predicted 2.447 and returned 2.202. A one-coordinate x2 move in round 12
returned 2.390, the final best.

**Result.** Best 2.390 at **0.002673-0.261928-0.255873-0.290302-0.327805-0.603094**. Confidence: low that this is
the maximum. F7 was still improving in the last scored round.

### Function 8 — ML model, eight hyperparameters (8D)

**Overview.** 8D input; 40 initial points; output is a model performance score.

**Nature of the data.** Smooth and noise-free in practice. Three inputs (x3, x1, x7) carry about 70% of the
GP relevance. Coverage is thin: the nearest data point to a random input is typically 0.6 away.

**Strategy.** GP-UCB; single-coordinate quadratic closures (dims 5, 6, 4 and 8); two multi-coordinate GP
moves, both of which failed; then one coordinate at a time.

**Learning.** A PCA of my own F8 queries showed most of my sampling went along dims 5 and 6, two of the
least relevant inputs. Single-coordinate predictions were accurate to about 0.003.

**Result.** Best 9.930 at **0.198086-0.192416-0.216581-0.138786-0.800003-0.350000-0.198013-0.793085**. Confidence:
moderate that this is the local maximum, where the remaining single-coordinate gains are below 0.01. It is
not necessarily global, given the thin coverage in eight dimensions.
